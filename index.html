About this data

Two initiatives answered the pilot form on November 5, 2026: Andel Koester for NYCE's Climate Data Stewardship Initiative, and Brittany Janis for Public Environmental Data Partners. Their points are their own answers, with one label adjusted: NYCE's answer "Data Stewardship" is plotted as the form's "Data Preservation & Stewardship."

The other nine initiatives are Steve Diggs's estimates, translated from the earlier first-pass placements into the form's categories. Each one is replaced when that initiative responds.

The three axes

These are the pilot form's own categories. Functions are ordered along the data lifecycle, from collection through use, followed by the work that supports the whole chain, so the axis reads left to right.

        What the first two responses show

        • NYCE describes its initiative more broadly than the earlier estimate did.The earlier placement had NYCE's initiative serving researchers through preservation and coordination. NYCE's own answer names decision makers and data users, and puts data access and usability first among its functions, alongside stewardship and prioritization. It doesn't name network coordination.
        • PEDP places itself in federal and environmental justice data, and doesn't claim advocacy.PEDP's answer names Federal and Environmental Health & Justice, with access, tool development, and network coordination as its functions. The estimate for EDGI, one of PEDP's three leads, does include advocacy, so the two may divide that work between them.
        • Both respondents meet in one cell.NYCE and PEDP each named Data Access & Usability, the Data Resilience Community, and Data Users. Neither named data collection, standards, repositories, or integration, which leaves the lifecycle's early stages to the estimates for now.

        Questions about the form

        • Is "Federal" a discipline?Federal describes where data comes from, not what field it belongs to. PEDP chose it, and it fits PEDP, but it sits on the same axis as Energy and Physical Sciences. A separate question on data origin (federal, state, academic, community) would keep the disciplines axis to one kind of thing.
        • Did the form cap the number of choices?Both respondents chose exactly three stakeholders and exactly three functions. If the form limits answers to three, broad initiatives will look narrower than they are, and the cap should be stated or lifted.
        • Should answers match the form's labels exactly?NYCE's "Data Stewardship" doesn't match any of the 13 function labels. Fixed checkboxes would prevent the mismatch.
        • Should the form ask what's core and what's incidental?The earlier draft drew home domains and reach differently. The form doesn't ask which answers are central to an initiative's mandate, so the cube can't show that distinction from responses.
        • Should the form ask about funding?Funder type comes from Steve Diggs's notes, not the form. A single question on who pays (federal, philanthropy, host institution, volunteers and small grants, unfunded) would let the funder view run on reported data.
